# What I expect to happen: delayed, uneven fiscal adjustment

September 18, 2026. Records the assistant's assessment from the September 16 project
discussion, at the project owner's request.

**My central expectation is years of continued borrowing, increasingly painful
tradeoffs, and several rounds of reluctant adjustment.** I expect the United States
to keep servicing Treasury debt while households absorb costs through a combination
of taxes, reduced public benefits and services, expensive credit, and episodes of
lost purchasing power.

This is a qualitative judgment informed by the project and the evidence cited below.
The simulator has not established this sequence, its timing, or its probability.
The fiscal projections cited here retain their stated publication vintages; this
note does not refresh the model's inputs.

## Why borrowing can continue while the problem worsens

Investors can keep lending while expecting future governments to change policy.
They do not have to believe that today's tax and spending arrangements will last
forever. My interpretation is that this leaves politicians considerable room to
postpone decisions. The project never established where that room ends.

Postponement nevertheless becomes costly. CBO's February 2026 baseline projects
federal net interest rising from 3.3% of GDP in 2026 to 4.6% in 2036. That spending
must be financed through revenue, additional borrowing, or reductions elsewhere.
CBO also identifies higher borrowing costs, reduced private investment, and slower
output growth as consequences of growing debt. These effects are relative to a
path with less debt; they do not imply that every market interest rate rises every
year. [CBO, *The Budget and Economic Outlook: 2026 to 2036*](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105)

I therefore expect pressure to reach mortgages, business investment, wages, and
budget negotiations well before a lasting inability to sell Treasury securities.
Continuing to make bond payments is compatible with worsening choices for households.

## Politics eventually responds, but late and incompletely

My expectation is repeated compromises that protect current recipients, shift more
costs onto future taxpayers and beneficiaries, and borrow to bridge the remaining
gaps. I would not assume a single comprehensive agreement that permanently settles
the issue.

There are concrete pressure points before any hypothetical market crisis. The
2026 Social Security trustees report projects that the Old-Age and Survivors
Insurance trust fund will exhaust its reserves in the fourth quarter of 2032.
Continuing income would then cover 78% of scheduled benefits. That projection
concerns the retirement and survivors fund, not the disappearance of Social Security
or the exhaustion of both trust funds together. It creates a legislative
confrontation independently of Treasury investors' willingness to lend.
[Social Security Administration, June 9, 2026](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/press/releases/2026-06-09.html)

Congress could authorize additional financing. My guess is that some such postponement
is more likely than accepting the full scheduled-benefit shortfall without a response.
Financing a transfer with additional federal borrowing would move the burden onto
the wider budget; it would not remove the underlying gap.

I do not expect every compromise to arrive calmly. A recession, inflation shock,
or sharp increase in borrowing costs could force decisions under worse conditions.
The IMF's 2026 U.S. assessment identifies the fiscal trajectory as a growing risk
to financial stability, including through the Treasury market's global importance.
[IMF, 2026 Article IV assessment](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/01/pr-26102-usa-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consult)

My inference is that episodes of stress are more likely to produce emergency
interventions and partial fiscal changes than one definitive resolution. This
does not assume that politicians enact a preventive rescue before economic pain
changes their incentives.

## Inflation is a risk, not the automatic ending

I would substantially downgrade the project's dramatic inflation scenario as a
forecast. In the paired stress experiments, changing unestimated quarterly inflation
persistence from 0.98 to 0.90 lowered annualized quarterly GDP-price inflation in
2056 from 8.04% to 2.89%. Debt still exceeded 250% of GDP in both cases. That is
strong evidence of sensitivity within the model, not evidence that either coefficient
describes the U.S. economy. [Project sustainability review](sustainability_review.md)

I expect political pressure to tolerate inflation when controlling it becomes
painful. But sustained high inflation would depend on monetary and fiscal choices
and economic behavior beyond the mere existence of government interest payments.
Our work did not establish that the interest-income channel dominates the other
effects of higher rates.

Inflation also does not cleanly erase the financing problem. The project's Treasury
ledger captures an important limitation: fixed coupons persist until refinancing,
shorter debt reprices sooner, and inflation-linked principal adjusts. Unexpected
inflation can reduce the real value of existing nominal obligations, while subsequent
borrowing can become more expensive. Some public commitments also adjust with prices.
[Project article and technical appendix](continuing_to_borrow.pdf)

If governments refuse meaningful fiscal adjustment even after substantial economic
pain, and monetary policy accommodates the resulting pressure, I would place much
more weight on a sustained inflationary outcome. That requires additional assumptions
about policy. It is not the central expectation recorded here.

## Who bears the consequences

My expected distribution of costs is uneven:

- **Younger workers and future beneficiaries:** greater uncertainty about taxes,
  retirement benefits, and eligibility rules, with more of the adjustment deferred
  onto them.
- **People dependent on public services:** tighter eligibility, reduced provision,
  or deterioration in services as budget compromises accumulate.
- **Borrowers and workers:** financing costs that restrain housing and business
  investment, with consequences for employment and wage growth relative to a
  healthier fiscal path.
- **Holders of fixed nominal claims:** purchasing-power losses when inflation
  exceeds what was anticipated. Some debtors can benefit from the same surprise;
  these losses are not uniform across households.

This is an assessment of likely channels, not a household incidence calculation
performed by the simulator. Aggregate living standards could continue to rise
while rising more slowly and becoming less secure for particular groups.

## How much confidence to place in this view

I am more confident about accumulating pressure and uneven adjustment than about
the precise mix of taxes, spending changes, inflation, and growth. The project
supports the accounting mechanisms and exposes the sensitivity of particular stress
stories. It has not identified a U.S. breaking point or supplied an evidence-based
probability ranking of competing endings.

A sustained improvement in productivity and fiscal balances would make this outlook
less adverse. Persistent financing stress combined with refusal to adjust budgets
and weakening inflation control would make it more adverse. Those are reasons to
revise the assessment, not thresholds estimated by this model.

My central expectation is that politics eventually bends, but late, unevenly, and
after avoidable damage.
